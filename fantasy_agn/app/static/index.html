How to cite FANTASY

If you publish results obtained with FANTASY, please cite the FANTASY paper and the two papers it builds on:

  1. Ilić, D., Rakić, N. & Popović, L. Č. 2023, ApJS, 267, 19 · the FANTASY paper
    ADS · doi:10.3847/1538-4365/acd783
  2. Rakić, N. 2022, MNRAS, 516, 1624
    ADS · doi:10.1093/mnras/stac2259
  3. Ilić, D. et al. 2020, A&A, 638, A13
    ADS · doi:10.1051/0004-6361/202037532

For example: “The spectra were fitted with FANTASY (Ilić et al. 2020; Rakić 2022; Ilić, Rakić & Popović 2023).”

If you fit the host galaxy with stellar templates, cite them too (E-MILES: Vazdekis et al. 2016); fa.templates.TEMPLATE_SETS gives the reference of each template set.

A real fit, replayed

From the light of a galaxy to the black hole at its heart.

One SDSS fiber, one spectrum of a Seyfert 1. FANTASY fits the starlight of the host galaxy, the accretion disk, Fe II and the broad and narrow lines together, and weighs the black hole.

    Initial guess
    The same in all epochs, or one per epoch?fa.fit_epochs(spectra, model, shared=…)

    In the joint fit a shared quantity has one value for all epochs; the others are fitted in every epoch. The shared fluxes are what the flux scale of each epoch is measured on, so they must be lines that do not vary (the narrow lines; the host). A shared shape (shift, width, profile) says the line looks the same in every epoch and only its flux changes. These are assumptions about the object: set them here.

    Compare models on this spectrumfa.fit · BIC = χ² + k ln n

    Fit

    fa.fit(spec, model)

    First, try the model on one epoch r = fa.fit(spectra[k], model)

    Fit one epoch alone and look at the result: the residuals, the checks, the line profiles. Change the model and try again until it is right for this object. Then fit all epochs with it; they start from this solution. A second look at the faintest epoch shows whether the model is too rich for the weak ones.

      fa.analyze_residuals(r)

      Finds coherent features in the residuals (missing lines, wings, absorption, continuum shape, sky residuals), identifies them and suggests a change to the model you can apply.

      Lines to add or separatefa.suggest_lines(r)

      fa.robustness(r)

      Refits the spectrum with each width, shift and ratio fixed at values across its limits (a few hundred fits, about 1–3 minutes). It shows which parameters the data really measure, whether another solution fits almost as well, and how much your results change between fits that are all acceptable.

      fa.compare_fits(a, b)

      Two fits can describe the spectrum equally well and still give different results, because components trade flux where they overlap. Pin this fit, change the model (continuum, line profile, ties) and fit again, then compare: both fits, where the flux went, and both broad-line profiles.

      fa.prune_feii(r)

      Every Fe II group has its own strength, which cannot be negative, so noise can only push a weak group up: Fe II comes out too high, the continuum too low and the broad line beside it too strong. This fixes the groups below the threshold at zero and fits again. Use it for weak Fe II or low S/N. With moderate Fe II it also removes real weak groups (in simulations at RFe II = 0.25 the Fe II flux came out 13% too low instead of 6% too high), so say in your results that you used it.

      fa.simulation_check(r)

      Takes the fitted model as the truth, draws spectra from it with the noise of your spectrum and fits each one again from scratch, exactly as your spectrum was fitted (60 fits, about a minute). It shows whether a result comes back too high or too low on average, and whether its error bar is the right size. It tests the fitting procedure for this model at this S/N; it cannot see what the model itself leaves out (checks 1 and 3).

      fa.calibration_check(me) · fa.simulation_check(me)

      Calibration check: fits every epoch again on its own, divided by the flux scale of the joint fit, with everything free. If the scales are right and the narrow lines really the same in every epoch, their fluxes agree within the errors. More scatter is calibration noise the error bars do not contain (extended narrow lines seen through different apertures, resolution or wavelength scale differing between epochs). With one strong narrow line the scales are set by it, so it agrees almost by construction; the test has power through the other lines and the line widths.

      Simulations: draws sets of epochs from the joint fit with their noise, fits each set jointly again exactly as yours, and compares the flux scales, the light curve and the shared parameters with the truth (each set is a whole joint fit: minutes).
