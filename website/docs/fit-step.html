FANTASY 2.1.0 / The app, step by step

4 · Fit

Check what will be fitted, choose how epochs are treated and how uncertainties are computed, then run the fit.

Several epochs

First, try the model on one epoch
Fit this epoch fits one epoch alone (by default the one of highest S/N; any epoch can be chosen) with the current model: r = fa.fit(spectra[k], model). The Results step then shows that fit with all its checks (residuals, robustness, compare, weak Fe II, simulations), and a line that leads back: Change the model, or Fit all epochs. Repeat until the model is right for the object; a look at the faintest epoch shows whether it is too rich for the weak ones.
Start every epoch from the trial fit
shown once an epoch was tried: the epochs are fitted with model.starting_from(r), so each also starts from the values of the trial fit and keeps the better of that and its own data-driven guess. The limits, fixed values and ties are those of the model. If the model was changed after the trial, the parameters it still shares with the trial are used.
The same in all epochs, or one per epoch?
set in the Model step of a multi-epoch analysis, in the table under the component: for every component, its fluxes and its shape (shift, width, profile parameters, slope) are the same in all epochs or fitted per epoch. The shared fluxes are what the flux scale of each epoch is measured on, so they must not vary: the narrow lines and a stellar host by default. A shared shape says the line looks the same in every epoch and only its flux changes: the default for the broad lines and Fe II, since free shapes per epoch trade flux with the continuum and can wash out the reverberation signal. Everything else (the continuum, the outflow wing) is per epoch by default. These are assumptions about the object; changed marks what differs from the default, Defaults restores it, and the Fit step repeats the choice in one line. In code: fa.fit_epochs(spectra, model, shared=("narrow.*", "broad.fwhm", ...)). For a stellar host the flux choice is the same or amplitude per epoch: one host spectrum with its own amplitude in every epoch, for spectra taken through different apertures or in different seeing (epoch_scaled=("host",)).
Together (recommended)
fa.fit_epochs: one joint fit, narrow lines (and a stellar host) shared, one flux scale per epoch fixed by the shared lines (intercalibration). Best for light curves and reverberation mapping.
Each on its own
fa.fit_many: independent fits in parallel, with the calibration of each spectrum as it is.
Wavelength scale of the epochs
fit a small offset per epoch (recommended; fa.fit_epochs(..., wavelength_offsets=True)): every epoch gets a velocity of up to ±150 km/s, added to all the shifts of the model in that epoch, with the reference epoch at 0. The shared narrow lines measure it, as they measure the flux scale. The epochs are never on exactly the same wavelength scale: the calibration differs by a few km/s (tens for slit spectra), and when the files carry slightly different redshifts (SDSS: about 20 km/s rms) each spectrum is moved to the rest frame with its own; the note under the choice says by how much they differ. In simulations, unmodelled offsets of 30 km/s raised the error of the flux scales from 4% to 17%; with the offsets fitted it stayed at 3%, and they were recovered to 3 km/s. The table of all epochs lists the offsets, and the Results tile Redshift, narrow lines gives the redshift of the object from the narrow lines of all epochs together (me.redshift()), with the rms of the offsets. as prepared fits none.
Resolution of the epochs
fit an extra broadening per epoch (fa.fit_epochs(..., epoch_broadening=True)): every epoch gets an extra line width, a FWHM added in quadrature to every line width and to the stellar dispersion (up to 300 km/s; an epoch may also be sharper than the reference, down to 80% of its own resolution), measured on the shared narrow lines. The resolution differs between epochs (slit width, seeing, another spectrograph) and is often not known well; when it differs and is not modelled, the shared narrow lines fit some epochs badly and pull their flux scales. In simulations with up to 250 km/s of extra broadening the flux scales were off by up to 7%; with the broadening fitted, below 1%, and the broadening was recovered to about 10 km/s. Each epoch's widths are then intrinsic (its spectrum carries the broadened resolution); the table of all epochs lists the broadening and the Results tile gives its range. as prepared fits each epoch at the resolution of its spectrum.
Flux reference epoch
the epoch whose flux scale is 1; by default the one of highest S/N, since its uncertainty enters every other epoch's calibration.
Calibration between epochs
one flux scale per epoch; scale and tilt; or scale, tilt and curvature (calibration_degree 0, 1, 2): see Multi-epoch fitting.

For a sample, Model for each spectrum is automatic (classify each and use the recommended model; a spectrum that ends too blue for Hβ gets Mg II or C IV) or one model for all. The fit uses the prepared spectra, including individual row changes. For a large local sample, workers apply the common preparation to files beyond the preview. Choosing one median redshift uses the previewed spectra to determine that value. A rest-wavelength model requires a rest-frame spectrum or a known zero redshift. Check the error column for rows that could not be fitted. In Results, choose Download → Sample catalog (CSV) for one row per input.

Advanced options

Scale errors
inflate the errors by √χ2ν when χ2ν > 1.
Model uncertainty
fit the alternative decompositions and add their spread as a systematic error (Model comparison).
Absorption troughs
find troughs in the residuals and refit with them (Absorption troughs).
Rescale the errors of each epoch
by √χ2ν of its own fit, for epochs whose errors are under- or overestimated.
Shared parameters by name pattern
for experts: patterns such as narrow.*, feii.fwhm that replace the table of the Model step (below) when the field is not empty.
Processes
parallel processes for independent fits and Monte Carlo.

The run button is in the bar at the bottom of the page, between ← Model and See the results →, so it is always in view; it is the highlighted button until a fit of the current settings exists, then See the results is. When the fit is done, its quality and the parameters that ended at a limit are listed, with a link to the component in the Model step.
