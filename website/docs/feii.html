FANTASY 2.0.2 / Modelling

Fe II templates

Fe II emits thousands of blended lines. FANTASY models them as groups of lines that share a lower energy level: within a group the intensities follow atomic physics, between groups the strengths are free.

Lines from the same lower term change together from object to object, because their strength depends on how many ions sit in that (metastable) level. Within a group the relative intensities are those of a population at T = 104 K (Kovačević et al. 2010):

Iik ∝ gi fik λik−3 exp(−EkkT)

with gi the statistical weight of the lower level, fik the oscillator strength and Ek the upper-level energy. All lines share one shift and one FWHM (by default tied as in the presets).

Fit the UV model over a wide window (2200–3300 Å) and mask intervening absorbers: in a narrow window around Mg II the pseudo-continuum is degenerate with the power law.

Weak Fe II

The strength of every group is free and cannot be negative, so noise moves a weak or absent group only upwards: the summed Fe II comes out too high, the continuum too low and the neighbouring broad line too strong. In simulations of an SDSS quasar at S/N 30 (presets.hbeta()) the Fe II flux was 9% too high at RFe II = 0.25, 66% at 0.08, and with no Fe II at all the fit returned RFe II = 0.12 and a broad Hβ flux 12% too high. With strong Fe II or high S/N the effect is below 2%.

r = fa.fit(spec, fa.presets.hbeta())
r = fa.prune_feii(r)        # groups below 2 sigma fixed at 0, then fitted again
r.feii_pruning              # group, amplitude, error, kept or dropped

fa.prune_feii removes most of that bias where it is large (broad Hβ with no Fe II: +15% → +1%). Where Fe II is moderate it drops real weak groups too: at RFe II = 0.25 the Fe II flux came out 13% too low instead of 6% too high. It is a choice for weak Fe II and low S/N, not a default; state it with the results.
