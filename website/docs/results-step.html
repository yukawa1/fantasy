5 · Results

The summary at the top gives the fit quality and the main numbers with errors; parameters at a limit are flagged with a button that opens their component. A joint fit adds Flux scale between epochs: the median error of the flux scale of an epoch (me.calibration_error), marked when it is above 2%, since this noise enters every light curve.

Views

Fit
data, model, components and residuals (drag to zoom, double-click to reset; click the legend to hide a curve). Below, four tables: Lines (by component, with flags for undetected lines and tied widths; Hide undetected), Continuum and host (continuum, luminosities, host properties and the black-hole mass of every recipe that applies), All parameters (value, error, limits, status) and All epochs.
Degeneracies
which parameters trade against each other (r.cov, or r.samples after MCMC). The free parameters sit around a wheel, grouped by component; a ribbon joins two whose correlation |ρ| is at least the threshold, red when they move together, blue when one goes up as the other goes down, wider for a stronger one. A dot's size is the relative error, and a red ring marks a parameter at a limit. By default only pairs between components are shown (the ones that decide the decomposition); all pairs adds those within a component (width against flux of one line). Hover a parameter for its partners, a ribbon for the pair: the side panel gives the 68% and 95% regions of the two together and why they are linked. A thin diagonal ellipse (|ρ| ≥ 0.9) means the data fix only a combination of the two: report their sum, tie one, or treat each error as optimistic.
Atlas
(a sample, or several epochs) every fit as a small card: the data in grey, the model in the colour of its class (for epochs, of its date, early to late), the broad lines and the host shaded, the residuals in σ underneath. Order by puts the cards in order of a number (for a sample χ²ν, residual structure, redshift, broad FWHM, RFe II, MBH, Eddington ratio, luminosity, [O III]/Hβ; for epochs the date, continuum luminosity, broad-line flux, FWHM, fit quality), and the strip above places every fit on that axis. Worth a look keeps the fits flagged for χ²ν > 2, a run of 40 pixels more than 10σ off the model (noise alone gives about 4σ), a broad width at a limit, RFe II > 3, a black-hole mass outside 105.5–1010.5, or three or more parameters at limits; hover a flag for the reason. Common wavelength axis lines the lines up down the grid, same flux scale (on for epochs) shows the variability. The class chips filter a sample (alt-click: one class only). A card opens its fit in Fit. The Eddington ratio uses bolometric corrections of 9.26 at 5100 Å and 5.15 at 3000 Å.
Anatomy
the fit taken apart (r.model_flux(components=True)): the components stacked from the continuum up (host, Balmer continuum, Fe II, broad, narrow, outflow), with the data over them. The flux budget above gives the share of each in the flux of the range shown; hovering the spectrum gives the composition at that wavelength (a ring and a table, with data, model and residual). Peel layers takes components off the top and shows the data minus them: what is left for the rest to explain (peel the lines to see whether the continuum and Fe II follow the data). Below, the residual (data − model)/σ with the ±1σ band, ±3σ lines and a running mean over 15 pixels: noise averages out, a component the model misses stays as a bump; the count of pixels beyond 3σ is compared with what noise alone gives. Drag to zoom, double-click for the full range, the windows (Hβ, Hα, ...) above; the strip at the bottom shows the whole spectrum. Click a legend chip to hide a component, hover it (or click a budget segment) to isolate it.
Checks
five checks, numbered in the order to use them. Each is one line with its run button and a status (not run, nothing found, 3 features, 2 biased, ...); click its title to open or close it. A check that needs attention, or that is running, is open; the others are closed until you open them. The number on the Checks tab counts the checks that need attention. 1. Does the model miss anything?: the residual analysis (fa.analyze_residuals, threshold 3–6σ) marks missing lines, wings, absorption, continuum shape and sky residuals, suggests a change for each, and Refit with the changes applies the ones you keep. For one spectrum it also shows Lines to add or separate at once, without running anything (fa.suggest_lines(r), a few milliseconds): every library line the model lacks is tested as a Gaussian with the fitted width and shift of a line component the model already has (forbidden and coronal lines with the narrow and outflow components, permitted lines also with the broad ones), its best flux and the χ² it removes computed directly on the residuals (no refit), scaled by the scatter of the residuals. A line is listed when it lowers the BIC by more than 10 (one parameter: its flux) at S/N ≥ 3; lines are taken one at a time, each subtracted before the next is judged, so a blend is not counted twice. Lines on a line the model already has, on night-sky lines or covered by the Fe II and high-order Balmer templates are not tested. Add & refit adds the line and fits again in one cell, with the options of the last fit, and you stay on Results: the code is one fa.Lines per kinematic source, its shift and FWHM tied to that component, then r = fa.fit(spec, model). The same list tests the lines the model has: all lines of a component share one velocity, and a line (with its doublet) whose velocity differs, typically [O III] blueshifted by a few tens of km/s against the other narrow lines, leaves an excess on one side and a deficit on the other. The residuals projected on the velocity derivative of the line measure its offset; when its own shift and width lower the BIC by more than 10 (two parameters), the row says by how much, and Separate & refit moves it to a component of its own (model.split_lines; fluxes, ratios, limits and ties go with it). The residual analysis gives the same suggestion for a feature on such a line. Apply all & refit does every change of the list at once; after the trial fit of a multi-epoch analysis, only that epoch is fitted again. The list then comes from the new fit. 2. How robust is this fit? (fa.robustness(r), one spectrum): Check robustness fixes each free width, shift and ratio at values across its limits and refits everything else, from the best fit and from a fresh guess (a few hundred fits, 1–3 minutes). It shows your results (broad-line FWHM, σ, flux and shift, RFeII, total [O III]) over all acceptable fits (Δχ² ≤ 1 and ≤ 4) next to their error bars, and the parameters to look at: weakly bounded, slides to its limit, second minimum, with a small Δχ² scan for each. If the scans find a better fit, Fit again from the better solution starts the model there. 3. Compare with another fit (fa.compare_fits(a, b)): Pin this fit keeps it under a name; change the model (continuum, line profile, ties), fit again and Compare with current. It shows whether the data can tell the two fits apart (RMS difference of the total models in units of the error, χ² on the same pixels), the results of both with the change in errors, a figure with both fits, where the flux went between the components and both broad profiles with their half-maximum crossings (PDF), and a warning when a broad profile has a shoulder at half maximum, so that its FWHM is fragile and σ is the safer width. 4. Is weak Fe II inflating the fit? (fa.prune_feii(r), one spectrum or epochs fitted one by one, with Fe II and errors): the table lists every Fe II group with its strength, error and whether it is detected (2σ). Drop undetected groups fixes the groups below the threshold (2σ or 3σ) at zero and fits again; the new fit becomes the result, and a table shows what changed (Fe II flux, RFe II, the broad line, the continuum, BIC). Prune again from the full fit applies another threshold to the original fit; Back to the fit with every group restores it. For epochs fitted one by one the card lists every epoch with its number of groups and how many are below 2σ; pruning runs on all epochs (fits = fa.prune_feii(fits)) and the light curves, lags and Main sequence tab then use the pruned fits. A group dropped in one epoch and kept in the next adds scatter to the Fe II light curve. A joint fit has no pruning step. For a sample, tick Drop undetected Fe II groups and fit again in the Fit step (fa.fit_sample(files, prune_feii=True)): the list gets a Fe II dropped column, and before pruning Fe II on the main sequence diagram shows where the spectra were with every group. Why and when: Fe II templates, Weak Fe II. 5. Would this fit recover the truth? (fa.simulation_check(r), one spectrum): Run simulations takes the fitted model as the truth, draws 30, 60 or 120 spectra from it with the noise of the spectrum and fits each again from scratch as the spectrum was fitted (about a minute for 60). For every result the table gives the value in the fit, the mean of the simulations, the bias in units of the scatter, the scatter (the real statistical error), the error bar the fits report and how often the truth lies within it (68% for correct errors). Biased: the mean differs from the truth by more than 0.3 of the scatter and by more than chance allows. If the fit was pruned of Fe II groups (check 4), every simulation is pruned too, so running it before and after shows whether pruning helped for this object. It tests the procedure for this model; what the model leaves out is for checks 1 and 3. 5 (joint fit). Is the calibration between the epochs right? Check the calibration (fa.calibration_check(me)) fits every epoch again on its own, its spectrum divided by the flux scale of the joint fit, with everything free. If the scales are right and the narrow lines really the same in every epoch, the fluxes of the shared lines agree within their errors: the table gives per line the χ2/dof of the epochs around their mean, its p-value, the scatter against the error and the excess, the scatter to add in quadrature for χ2/dof = 1: calibration noise that is not in the error bars. Epochs more than 3σ off are listed. With one strong narrow line the scales are set by it, so that line agrees almost by construction; the check has power through the other lines and the widths (in simulations it found unmodelled resolution differences with χ2/dof = 26, and passed once the broadening was fitted). Run simulations (fa.simulation_check(me, n=20)) draws sets of epochs from the joint fit with their noise, fits each set jointly again exactly as the data were, and compares the flux scales, the broad-line flux of every epoch (the light curve), the offsets and broadening, and the shared widths with the truth: the bias and the scatter of (fit − truth) in units of the error bar (0 and 1 for right error bars) and how often the truth is within one error bar. Each set is a whole joint fit, so it takes minutes.
Correlations
several epochs (joint or one by one). Opening the tab measures every epoch once (fa.epoch_table: broad flux, FWHM, σ, shift and EW of every broad line, the continuum and log L, RFe II, [O III]; all cores, about 20 s for 76 epochs). After that everything is immediate. The map shows the Spearman ρ of every pair of quantities, with the number where p < 0.05 and “=” where a pair is the same number twice; with many pairs some reach p < 0.05 by chance. Click a square (or choose the two quantities at the top) to see that relation with its fit: slope and intrinsic scatter by maximum likelihood with errors in both quantities, the 68% range of the line from a bootstrap of the epochs, fluxes, widths and EWs in log10 so that the slope is a power law. Hover over a point for its date and values, click it to leave that epoch out (Restore brings them back). Continuum earlier by (observed-frame days, the rest-frame lag × (1 + z)) sets each line epoch against the continuum interpolated that much earlier between the epochs (fa.correlations.shift_continuum), and the map and the fit follow as you drag; without it the quantities are taken at the same epoch, which biases the intrinsic Baldwin effect when the lag is comparable to the variability. Look for the intrinsic Baldwin effect (EW against luminosity), the breathing of the BLR (width against luminosity) and a constant [O III] (the flux calibration). PDF, PNG and SVG of the relation or of the grid of all pairs, and their Python code, use the current lag and left-out epochs.
Publication figure
ranges, title, journal and width (MNRAS, A&A, ApJ columns), zoom panels under the spectrum (quick Hβ, Hα, Mg II buttons; the whole fit, or with the continuum, Fe II and host subtracted), legend position (above, inside where it is free, beside), font size, line labels; for every kind of component whether it is drawn, its colour, fill, line width and style. PDF, PNG, EPS, SVG, and the Python code of the figure to copy. See Figures.
Line profiles
tick lines (broad lines are listed first) and choose Each in a panel (each line alone with its components, FWHM, σ and flux, the half-maximum points and residuals) or Overlaid (the profiles on one velocity axis, scaled to their peaks, to compare widths and shapes; with several epochs, Compare: Every epoch overlays the first chosen line of every epoch, in time order); a velocity range; PDF, PNG, EPS, SVG and the Python code.
Posterior
with Monte Carlo or MCMC errors: the corner plot of chosen parameters.
Main sequence
with Fe II and broad Hβ: RFe II against FWHM(Hβ) for every epoch; Zoom to the epochs (Fe II and the main sequence).
Classification
the final class, from the values of the fit (fa.classify_fit(r), after every fit, for every epoch): the broad lines of the fit and its narrow-line ratios and EW(Hα) on the BPT and WHAN diagrams, with the measurements. When the spectrum was classified in the Model step, that preliminary class is shown beside it (the table of epochs marks a disagreement with ≠), with its three steps (starlight, broad Hα, emission lines). Figures to download.
Light curves & lags
(joint fit, or epochs fitted one by one through fa.EpochSet(fits), which are not intercalibrated) for several epochs. Choose a driver and a responder (a continuum window, the fitted AGN continuum, the Fe II flux, or the flux, FWHM or EW of a broad line; the chips give common pairs such as 5100 Å → Hβ, or Hβ flux → FWHM for breathing; ⇄ swaps them). Both are shown as the % change from the mean of the epochs shown, with their fractional variability Fvar; the season buttons zoom to one observing season, hovering gives an epoch and clicking opens it. The τ slider (or a drag along the curve below it, or sweep) shifts the responder back by τ over the driver. The lag of the pair is measured with every method (fa.rm.ccf_lag, vn_lag, zdcf_lag, gp_lag; a few seconds per pair), and adopted lag picks the method whose own curve is drawn: the ICCF r(τ), live for the epochs shown; the ZDCF points (pyZDCF) with their errors; the von Neumann statistic, whose minimum is the lag (its axis runs downwards); or the DRW posterior. The handle and the readout follow that curve, the rows under it show each method's lag distribution, and clicking a method adopts it. Continuum windows (me.window_continuum()) are measured on the data in line-free rest-frame windows (Hβ: 4435–4700 and 5100–5535 Å; Hα: 6000–6250 and 6800–7000 Å; Shen et al. 2011) inside the fitted range: the mean of the intercalibrated flux minus the fitted lines and Fe II (the blue Hβ window lies on the Fe II 4570 blend). They include the host light, which dilutes the variability but not the lag; the fitted continuum is evaluated at one wavelength from the model. These lags use the two light curves alone. For the published lag, rms spectrum and mass, reverberation mapping: choose the line (the responder's, when it is a broad-line flux), the adopted method (primary, from the switch above), the lag methods (ICCF, von Neumann, ZDCF, DRW), an MJD range and detrending, then Measure lag (Reverberation mapping). The table of lags gives the significance of each: the share of its distribution (all Monte Carlo or posterior samples, aliases included) on its side of zero, as a one-sided Gaussian equivalent (fa.rm.lag_significance). A lag of 6.0 (−4.1, +4.9) days is about 1.3σ: not a detection. The warnings say when the adopted lag is below 2σ, when the light curves are weakly correlated (rmax < 0.5), when methods disagree by more than 2σ and when much of a distribution sits in seasonal aliases.

Downloads

The fitted lines (CSV or FITS), the model and its components (data, model, every component, host, AGN, spectrum minus host and, with extinction, the transmission and intrinsic components), the fit figures (PDF), the script, the notebook and the project.

Report is one HTML file of the current fit, to keep with the results or send to a colleague (it prints to PDF from the browser): the figure, the fit statistics and warnings, the key results, the lines (with upper limits), every check that was run on this fit with its outcome (residuals, robustness, comparison, Fe II pruning, simulations; for a joint fit the calibration check, its simulations and the lag), the settings of each step and the Python code that repeats the analysis.

In the lines table an undetected or weak line (below 2σ) shows an upper limit: r.line_limit(line), the fitted flux (if positive) plus 3σF, where σF is the flux error of a line with the fitted width and shift from the noise under it alone; with MCMC errors, the 95% limit of the posterior. Hover over it to see which.
