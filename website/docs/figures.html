FANTASY 2.0.2 / Figures

Figures

Every figure is drawn in one style: quiet grey axes with inward ticks on all sides, no grid, the model in ink over grey data, Fe II lightly filled, a ±1σ band behind the residuals, and colours that stay distinct for colour-blind readers.

The fit

r.plot()                                         # data, model, components, residuals
r.plot(xlim=(4600, 5100), label_lines="all", width="single")
r.plot(colors={"broad": "navy"}, hide=("host",), title=None)
r.plot(styles={"feii": {"fill": False, "ls": "--"},
               "broad": {"fill": True, "lw": 1.5},
               "model": {"lw": 0.6}})
r.plot(zoom=[(4650, 5150), (6350, 6800)])        # zoom panels of the Hβ and Hα regions under the spectrum
r.plot(zoom=[(4650, 5150)], zoom_subtract=("continuum", "feii"))   # ... with the continuum and Fe II subtracted
r.plot(width="mnras-double", legend="inside")    # a journal column; the legend where the plot is free
r.plot(style="sans-serif", height=0.8)           # sans-serif font; a compact (0.8) or tall (1.25) figure
r.savefig("fit.pdf")                             # PDF, EPS, SVG (editable text), PNG (300 dpi)
group
"auto": the sum of each kind and, when a line has several components of a kind, each of them (dashed, dotted); True only sums; False every component.
styles
per kind (data, model, continuum, host, feii, narrow, broad, outflow, …): fill, lw (points), ls ("-", "--", ":", "-.").
width
"single" (3.5 in, one journal column), "double" (7.1 in), a journal column ("mnras" 84 mm, "mnras-double" 174 mm, "aa" 88 mm, "aa-double" 180 mm, "apj", "apj-double") or inches.
legend
"above" (default), "inside" (where it covers the fewest data and line labels; above if nowhere is free), "right" or False.
zoom
wavelength ranges drawn again in a row of panels under the spectrum; the main panel marks them with a faint blue band and a letter. zoom_subtract: kinds subtracted from the data and the model in these panels ("continuum", "feii", "host"), so the lines stand alone.
label_lines
name the strongest or all fitted lines; label_options for font, size, direction, colour, connectors.

Single lines and grids

r.plot_line("Hb")                                # the line alone, its components and their sum
r.plot_line("Ha", others=True, velocity=False)
r.plot_lines(["Hg", "Hb", "Ha"], residuals=True) # a grid: 1, 2, 3 in a row, 4 as 2 × 2

A single-line panel shows the data with everything else in the model subtracted, each component with its FWHM, their sum (and the summed broad profile), the FWHM, line dispersion σ and flux of the line rounded to their errors, a bar between the half-maximum points and its residuals. A FWHM that a shoulder at half maximum makes fragile is marked; σ is then the safer width. Wide windows are labelled in 103 km/s.

r.plot_profiles(["Hb", "Ha"])                    # broad profiles overlaid, each scaled to its peak
fa.plotting.plot_profiles([r1, r2], "Hb", labels=["power law", "broken"])   # one line in two fits

The overlay compares the shapes of lines (is broad Hβ wider than Hα? more asymmetric?) or of one line in several fits or epochs; the legend gives FWHM and σ, the dots the half-maximum points. In the app: Results → Line profiles, tick the lines and choose Each in a panel or Overlaid. Both figure tabs show the Python code of the figure, to copy into a script.
