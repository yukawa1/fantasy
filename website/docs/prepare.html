2 · Prepare

Put every spectrum into the rest frame, correct the Milky Way extinction, set the flux unit and remove what the model should not see.

Redshift

from file, manual, or, with several spectra, one for all (median): every spectrum is moved with the median of the redshifts of the files (z = float(np.median([s.z for s in raw if s.z]))), the default for epochs of one object. Pipeline redshifts differ from file to file (SDSS: about 20 km/s rms), and moving each epoch with its own shifts the same line between epochs; the note under the choice says by how much the files differ. Move to rest frame applies it. Fits are done in the rest frame: spec.to_rest_frame(z) divides wavelengths by 1+z and multiplies flux densities by 1+z.

Galactic extinction

spec.deredden() divides by the Fitzpatrick (1999) transmission with RV = 3.1:

Fcorr(λ) = F(λ) · 10 0.4 E(B−V) k(λ)

E(B−V) comes from the file (DESI), the local SFD98 maps (sfdmap2 and SFD_DIR) or the IRSA dust service (this sends the coordinates to NASA/IPAC), recalibrated by 0.86 (Schlafly & Finkbeiner 2011) unless sf11=False. In the app: From RA/Dec (the position of the file, or typed RA and Dec in degrees or sexagesimal), Enter value, or None. A spectrum is never corrected twice.

spec = spec.with_coordinates("12:34:56.7 +12:34:56").deredden()
At high Galactic latitude E(B−V) = 0.015–0.06: without the correction fluxes and L5100 are 5–20% too low and the Balmer decrement 2–6% too high, while ratios of nearby lines (BPT) change by < 0.003 dex.

Flux unit

Luminosities and black-hole masses need the flux unit. SDSS and DESI give 10−17 erg s−1 cm−2 Å−1; a text file may say so in its header (“flux in 1e-16 erg/s/cm2/A”). Otherwise set it in One flux unit of the file is (10−15–10−19 or other) or with spec.with_flux_unit(1e-16).

Finding the redshift

Find the redshift (fa.find_redshift(spec)) is for a spectrum without a redshift, and a check of one that has it. The spectrum as read is fitted with galaxy and quasar templates (Yip et al. 2004) and a smooth polynomial at every redshift; the minimum of χ² is the redshift, as in the SDSS and DESI pipelines. A quasar redshift is then refined on the narrow Hβ and [O III] lines when they are detected. It takes a few seconds.

The panel shows the best redshift with its error, secure or not secure, and the candidates: the best minima of both template sets, each with how much worse it fits (χ² per pixel) and how many of the emission peaks of the spectrum sit on a known line there. Click a candidate to use it. The figure shows χ² against redshift and the spectrum with the template and the lines of the best redshift marked. A wrong redshift is nearly always one line taken for another, so look at the candidates when the answer is not secure.

On 540 SDSS spectra of nine classes (z = 0.01–2.5, median S/N 14) the best redshift was right for 97%; of those called secure (96%), 99.8% were right. Galaxies and AGN below z ≈ 1.5 were right in 98–100% of the cases; quasars above z ≈ 1.7 in 83–90%, with the right value among the candidates for 95%. It cannot identify a single line in a narrow range, nor featureless spectra; the galaxy templates reach z ≈ 1 in an optical spectrum; stars are not included.

Instrumental resolution

The instrument broadens every line; FANTASY removes that width in quadrature, so fitted widths are intrinsic. SDSS and DESI files carry the resolution along the spectrum. Text and plain FITS files do not: the widths then include the instrument, and a narrow line can become narrower than a pixel. In The lines are broadened by the instrument by choose FWHM in km/s (of an unresolved sky or arc line) or resolving power R (R = 2000 is 150 km/s); the note shows the pixel size to compare with. In code: spec.with_resolution(150) or spec.with_resolution(R=2000).

After a fit, A line narrower than the data can sample on the Results page lists widths below 1.2 pixels (r.undersampled()). Line profiles are averaged over each pixel, as the spectrograph records them, so the flux of such a line is kept wherever its peak falls; but a width below about a pixel barely changes the data, so it is poorly measured, and the line can fit single noisy pixels. Set the resolution, or raise the lower limit of the width.

Range and masks

Wavelength range
the fitted range (rest frame); presets set a sensible default.
Telluric bands, sky lines
spec.mask_sky(): the telluric A, B and H2O absorption bands and the strongest night-sky emission lines (observed frame).
Narrow absorption
spec.mask_absorption(): finds intervening absorbers (Mg II, C IV doublets, …) and masks them; essential in the UV.
Ranges on the plot
drag on the spectrum to mask a range (spec.mask((5570, 5585))).
Apply to
with several epochs, change one epoch only.

The plot shows the Raw or the Prepared spectrum of the chosen Epoch, with the masked ranges hatched.

In Python

spec.crop(4000, 7000)          # restrict the range
spec.subtract_host()           # remove the host with galaxy + quasar eigenspectra (Yip et al. 2004)
spec.to_air()                  # vacuum to air wavelengths
